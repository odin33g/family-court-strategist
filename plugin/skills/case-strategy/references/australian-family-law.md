# Australian Family Law — Quick Reference for Case Strategy

> **Law current as at June 2026.** Reflects the Family Law Amendment Act 2023 (commenced 6 May 2024) and Family Law Amendment Act 2024 (commenced 10 June 2025). Before building strategy on any provision, verify it is still current — and warn the user if their existing documents cite pre-2024 law (old s60CC primary/additional structure, s61DA presumption, s65DAA equal time — all repealed).

## Best Interests Test (s60CC) — six general considerations, non-hierarchical

1. **s60CC(2)(a)** — Safety of the child and carers (incl. s60CC(2A): history of family violence/abuse/neglect and any FV orders)
2. **s60CC(2)(b)** — Child's views — AND what shaped them (challenge here if influence/coaching suspected)
3. **s60CC(2)(c)** — Child's developmental, psychological, emotional and cultural needs
4. **s60CC(2)(d)** — Each carer's capacity to meet those needs (includes supporting the child's important relationships)
5. **s60CC(2)(e)** — Benefit of relationship with both parents and significant others, **where safe** (critical if contact is blocked without safety justification)
6. **s60CC(2)(f)** — Anything else relevant
+ **s60CC(3)** — Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander children: culture and connection

Safety is listed first. If the other side argues safety, test the evidence. If the evidence shows the child is safe with the user, blocking the relationship promotes nothing in this list.

## Parental Responsibility (post-2024)

The s61DA **presumption of equal shared parental responsibility is REPEALED**, and so is the s65DAA equal-time pathway. Never cite either. Current framework:
- s61CA — parents encouraged to consult on major long-term issues, where safe
- s61D — court allocates parental responsibility (sole/joint/split) on best interests alone
- s61DAA — if joint decision-making is ordered, parties must consult and make a genuine effort to agree
- s61DAB — no consultation needed for day-to-day decisions during the user's care time

Time arrangements are argued directly on s60CC factors — chiefly (2)(e) benefit of the relationship and (2)(d) capacity — not via any presumption.

## The Old "Willingness to Facilitate" Factor

Former s60CC(3)(c) (friendly parent factor) no longer exists. Map the same behaviour to current law instead:
- Contact obstruction without safety basis → (2)(e)
- Influencing the child's views → (2)(b)
- Failing to support the child's emotional needs/relationships → (2)(d)

## Variation Test — s65DAAA (Rice v Asplund codified)

Court must not reconsider final parenting orders unless it has considered whether there has been a **significant change in circumstances** and reconsideration is in the child's best interests. *Radecki & Radecki* [2024] FedCFamC1A 246: the court must make **findings of fact** about what changed — no finding, application fails. Pre-2024 Rice v Asplund case law still guides application.

Key arguments:
- Orders not being complied with
- Recommendations not implemented (e.g., family therapy ordered but never done)
- New information not available at time of original orders
- Child's relationship deteriorated without justification
- Material change in the child's needs or a parent's circumstances

For consent orders specifically:
- Consent was not truly informed
- Client was pressured (rushed timeline, inadequate advice)
- Evidence was never cross-examined
- Conditional terms were never met

⚠️ Warn the user: weak or repeated variation applications risk costs (s117) and harmful proceedings orders (Part XIB). Evidence first.

## Key Case Law

| Case | Principle | Use When |
|------|-----------|----------|
| Rice v Asplund (1979) | Significant change threshold — now codified s65DAAA | Filing variation application |
| Radecki & Radecki [2024] FedCFamC1A 246 | s65DAAA requires findings of fact on changed circumstances | Current authority for variations |
| CDJ v VAJ (1998) | Best interests paramount | Framing any argument |
| McCall & Clark (2009) | Investigate source of child's refusal | Child refusing contact |
| Maluka & Maluka (2011) | Caution re child's wishes if influenced | Influence indicators present |
| Goode & Goode (2006) | Interim hearing approach under OLD presumption | Treat with care — relies on repealed provisions |
| Mazorski & Albright (2007) | What a "meaningful relationship" involves | Describing relationship benefit under (2)(e); statutory term removed 2024 |

## Costs (s117)

Can be ordered against a party who: made false allegations, failed to comply with orders, acted unreasonably, prolonged proceedings unnecessarily.

## Other Current Provisions

- **s68LA(5A)** — ICL must meet the child (limited exceptions). ICL never met the child = documentable process failure.
- **Part XIB** — harmful proceedings orders (court can bar repeat filings without leave).
- **Part XIVB** — replaced old s121; publishing identifiable details of proceedings (incl. social media) is an offence. Warn users who mention posting about their case.
- **s4AB** — family violence definition; June 2025 reforms expanded economic abuse examples and added companion animals; property reforms also commenced June 2025.

## Relationship Interference (was: "Alienation in the Act")

No specific provision, and courts are cautious about the "parental alienation" label — advise users to plead **behaviour, not the label**. Captured through:
- s60CC(2)(e) — denial of the benefit of a safe relationship
- s60CC(2)(b) — influenced child's views (McCall & Clark; Maluka & Maluka)
- s60CC(2)(d) — capacity to meet the child's emotional needs
- s65DAAA — unexplained relationship deterioration as significant change

Look for in expert reports: adult language in child's statements, loyalty conflicts, conditional recommendations, professional observations about coaching — and whether recommendations were ever implemented.
